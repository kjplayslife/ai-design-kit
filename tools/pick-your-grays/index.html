A one-decision guide

Pick Your Grays

Pick one dark gray, or one white. Get the whole scale, the jobs for both modes, twelve contrast checks, and a globals.css snippet. The page re-themes itself with what you pick.

Step 1

Pick your brand color

One color. It's for buttons, links, and the one thing on each screen that matters most. It covers about 10% of the screen. Your gray takes its hue from it, so pick this first.

Every one of these passes white-text contrast. Pick by feeling, then nudge the sliders if you want.
Blue / indigo (220–250) reads as trust. Green / teal (150–180) reads as growth. Violet / orange (270, 20) reads as energy. Skip 45–95 (yellow, lime) and 0–12 (red).
Brand colors are 60–95% saturated. Under about 30% it reads as a tinted gray, not a brand. The exception is a near-black button (zinc-900).
This is your 600 step (light mode). 35–50% is the zone where white text passes 4.5:1. The 500 step for dark mode is derived: 8 points lighter, saturation pulled back 15%.

    Step 2

    Pick your starting gray

    Everything else comes from this one color. Dark first is easier: if your grays work on dark, light mode is a mirror.

    Rule 2: the gray keeps your brand hue at 3–10% saturation.
    Beginner shortcut. These already follow every rule. Build your own on project three.
    Blue / indigo / violet → ~220–250 (cool). Red / pink / none → ~240 at very low saturation (neutral). Green / orange / amber / brown → ~20–40 (warm).
    3–10% reads as gray. Above about 12% you can name the color at a glance, and it's too much. The scale keeps this saturation on every step, so judge it by the 500 swatch, not the dark anchor: Tailwind's slate-950 is 47% saturated and only gets away with it because it's nearly black.

    Rule check

      Step 3

      Build the scale

      Eleven steps from one color. Keep the hue. Keep the saturation. Only change lightness, using the ladder 5 · 10 · 16 · 25 · 33 · 46 · 64 · 83 · 90 · 96 · 98. The steps bunch up at the light end because the eye can tell light grays apart and you need many for backgrounds and borders.

      Step 4

      Give each step a job

      Dark mode uses six of the eleven. Light mode mirrors the jobs, not the hexes. Lighter means closer to the viewer on dark; on light, depth comes from border and shadow instead.

      How popular apps do it

      Same jobs, every time. Nobody uses pure mid-gray: GitHub leans blue, Material leans violet, Notion leans warm. Discord skips near-black and lifts the whole page. Apple uses true black and pays for it with three gray layers. Values are sampled from public design systems and UI; close, not exact. Click one to start from its page color.

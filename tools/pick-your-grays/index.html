A one-decision guide

Pick Your Grays

Nail down dark mode first: start from a palette you already like, dial in your gray, pick one brand color. Light mode is generated from it, and you tune it at the bottom. The page re-themes itself as you go.

Step 2

Dial in your dark gray

This one color is your page background (the 950 step). The other ten steps come from it by changing only lightness. Six rules: not black, a hint of hue at 3–10% saturation, text is the 50 step (not white), eleven steps that all differ, brand is lighter on dark, light mode mirrors the jobs.

Cool (blue, indigo, violet) → 220–250. Neutral → 240 at very low saturation. Warm (green, orange, amber, brown) → 20–40. Or press “Match gray hue to brand” after Step 3.
3–10% reads as gray. Above about 12% you can name the color, and it's too much. Every step keeps this value, so judge it by the 500 swatch, not the dark anchor.
Rule 1: not black. 3–8%. 0% is #000 and makes everything float. 10–14% is “lifted” like Lovable and Discord.
Rule 3: not pure white. 96–99%. This is your main text on dark and your muted fills on light.

    Step 3

    Pick your brand color

    One color, for buttons, links, and the one thing on each screen that matters most. You're picking how it looks on dark. The light-mode version is derived (a bit darker so white text passes) and you can nudge it at the bottom.

    Or skip this and use the sliders.
    Blue / indigo (220–250) reads as trust. Green / teal (150–180) reads as growth. Violet (270) and orange (20) read as energy. Skip 45–95 (yellow, lime) and 0–12 (red).
    Brand colors are 60–95% saturated. Under about 25% it becomes a neutral brand: a white button on dark, a black button on light.
    On dark, 50–65% pops without going neon. Pull saturation back a little if it glows.
    Rule 2: the gray keeps your brand hue at 3–10% saturation.

      Step 4

      Your scale

      Eleven steps from your gray, same hue and saturation, lightness from the ladder 5 · 10 · 16 · 25 · 33 · 46 · 64 · 83 · 90 · 96 · 98. The steps bunch up at the light end on purpose: the eye can tell light grays apart and you need many for backgrounds and borders.

      Step 5

      Dark mode, checked

      Six of the eleven steps get a job. Lighter means closer to the viewer, so cards are one step up from the page and there are no shadows. Six contrast pairs; text needs 4.5:1.

      Step 6

      Light mode, generated

      Light mode mirrors the jobs, not the hexes. White page, white cards with a border and a small shadow, your 950 step as text, your brand a bit darker so white text passes. Everything here comes from what you picked above. Tune only what looks off.

      Derived as your dark brand, 8 points darker and 10% more saturated. Slide left if white text on it is faint.
      500 passes 4.5:1 on white. 400 fails. Use 600 if you set helper text inside muted fills a lot.
      200 is two steps off white and reads as a line. 300 if your cards need to hold their edge on busy pages.
      100 is one step off white. 50 is barely there; use it when 100 looks dirty.
